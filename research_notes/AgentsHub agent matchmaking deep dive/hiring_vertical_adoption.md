## 1. Recruiting market size and structure in 2026, and the incumbents' AI moves

### Takeaway
Hiring is a large, concentrated market: roughly $37.6B in global online job advertising (LinkedIn and Indeed take about half), about $650B in global staffing, and an ATS/software layer of about $3B (narrow definition). The incumbents are monetizing AI matching aggressively. Indeed's US revenue per job rose about 39% YoY while posting volume stayed flat. However, every incumbent agent is either recruiter-side or a coaching tool inside a walled garden. None is an open interface between candidate agents and employer agents.

## 2. Candidate-side agents, the backlash, employer countermeasures, and the "AI doom loop"

### Takeaway
Application volume per job has roughly doubled since 2022 while recruiter teams shrank, and candidate trust is collapsing. Auto-apply tools are widespread but often don't work: in one May 2026 test, only 3 of 8 bots completed a submission. The tools are banned by LinkedIn and Indeed terms and are increasingly filtered by ATS anti-spam features. Both sides are now running unilateral AI against each other with no shared protocol. That is the core pain an agent-to-agent hub addresses.

### Cited Findings
- Greenhouse Benchmark (Mar 2026; 6,000+ orgs, 640M applications): applications per job rose 111%, from ~115 in 2022 to 244 in 2025. Annual applications per recruiter rose 411.8%, from 146 to 746 **[vendor data, large sample]** — [Greenhouse Benchmark Report, Mar 2026 (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/69c6a67c710b821d52b0d5d3_Greenhouse_Benchmark_Report_March2026_NorthAmerica.pdf). A secondary source adds that recruiting teams shrank about 55% over the same period **[secondary]** — [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/ai-auto-apply-bots/)
- Ashby: about 291 applications per hire, versus about 100 in 2021 (carried over from the earlier report, via Fortune) — [Fortune: Greenhouse CEO on the doom loop](https://fortune.com/2026/07/27/greenhouse-ceo-daniel-chait-ai-doom-loop-job-seekers-spam-interview-applications-unemployment/). I did not find the 2026 Ashby report itself.
- Greenhouse CEO Daniel Chait calls the dynamic a "doom loop": seekers use AI to apply everywhere, and recruiters use AI to filter — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/job-seekers-using-ai-apply-173021648.html)
- Trust: 46% of job seekers say their trust in hiring fell in the past year, many blaming AI (Greenhouse 2026 AI in Hiring Report) **[vendor]** — [Greenhouse 2026 AI in Hiring Report (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/69837ef69ad1bb87805ab84a_Greenhouse_C005_2026%20AI%20in%20Hiring%20Report_Client%20File.pdf). 63% of US seekers have been interviewed by AI, 70% weren't told beforehand, and 38% have quit a process over it **[vendor, via secondary]** — [Greenhouse blog](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/rebuilding-trust-in-hiring-how-to-create-transparent-candidate-experiences-in-the-age-of-ai); [Employer Branding News](https://employerbranding.news/resources/ai-in-hiring-statistics-2026-adoption-bias-trust-and-regulation/)
- Only 26% of candidates trust AI to evaluate them fairly. Sources **conflict on attribution** (Gartner vs Greenhouse's Candidate AI Interview Report). Greenhouse also reports that only 8% think AI makes hiring fairer — [Employer Branding News](https://employerbranding.news/resources/ai-in-hiring-statistics-2026-adoption-bias-trust-and-regulation/); [Apollo Technical](https://www.apollotechnical.com/statistics-on-ai-in-recruiting/)
- Employer side: a Robert Half survey (Mar 2026) of US hiring managers found 67% say AI-generated applications slowed hiring and 65% say they make skills harder to verify **[secondary summary]** — [CareerWin](https://careerwin.ai/blog/auto-apply-bots-getting-blacklisted); [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/ai-auto-apply-bots/)
- Auto-apply effectiveness: a May 2026 test found only 3 of 8 AI application bots actually completed submission. A 2026 field experiment found tailored AI CVs got more positive responses than generic ones (18% vs 10%) **[secondary]** — [HRLens](https://www.hrlens.io/articles/ai-auto-apply-bots-for-jobs); [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/ai-auto-apply-bots/)
- Countermeasures: Greenhouse and Ashby reportedly let employers cap repeat applications and block applicants flagged as spam. Ashby scores every applicant against recruiter criteria with cited evidence. Lever has fraud signals, including bot-like behaviour, and one pilot flagged 30 of 121 applicants. LinkedIn's user agreement bans third-party bots and uses behavioural detection. Indeed's terms bar automated bulk submission **[secondary]** — [Oaki: Is auto-apply safe](https://www.oaki.io/blog/is-auto-apply-safe); [Jobbie](https://jobbie.bot/blog/is-auto-apply-safe); [CareerWin](https://careerwin.ai/blog/auto-apply-bots-getting-blacklisted)
- No shared cross-employer blacklist exists. Claims of ATS "shadowbans" come mostly from marketing content — [Jobs AI Copilot](https://jobsaicopilot.com/does-auto-apply-get-you-blacklisted/)
- Fraud as a reason for identity checks: Lever's 30-of-121 suspicious flag above. The earlier report covers hiring-fraud regulation.

### Inferences
- The candidate-agent market already exists (LazyApply, Sonara, AIApply, Jobright and others), but it is aimed at web forms built for humans. Employers treat it as spam, and it partly fails technically. A hub where candidate agents are *sanctioned*, rate-limited and identity-verified flips the dynamic: auto-apply users become legitimate agent traffic. Auto-apply vendors are a natural *supply-side distribution partner*, though they carry reputational baggage.
- Employers' main pain is volume of unverifiable signal, not lack of candidates. The hub's employer value proposition should be "fewer, verified, structured, pre-negotiated candidates", not "more candidates".

### Gaps
- I found no reliable 2026 usage numbers (users or applications submitted) for LazyApply, Sonara, AIApply or Jobright. Sonara's 2024 shutdown and relaunch, and other vendor histories, were not verified this session.
- I did not obtain the Ashby 2026 Talent Trends report directly.

## 3. Candidate-agent to employer-agent protocols and interfaces, and public job data a hub could ingest

### Takeaway
No standard or vendor interface lets a candidate's agent formally apply to, or negotiate with, an employer's agent. Activity in 2026 is about *reading* jobs: many community and hosted MCP servers wrap the public Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Workday boards. Some ATS vendors reportedly expose recruiter-side MCP and A2A, which remains unverified. Public read APIs (Greenhouse Job Board, Lever Postings, Ashby Posting API) make seeding the employer side from public data technically easy.

### Cited Findings
- Read-only MCP servers for job discovery exist in quantity. Examples are `santex85/ats-jobs-mcp` (Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby public boards, plus ATS detection; it notes Ashby has no single-posting endpoint), Apify actors covering up to 8 ATSs including Workday, and Glama-listed "JobPostingsAPI" connectors. They are scrapers or aggregators, not vendor-sanctioned — [GitHub ats-jobs-mcp](https://github.com/santex85/ats-jobs-mcp); [Apify ATS Jobs MCP](https://apify.com/themineworks/ats-jobs/api/mcp); [Glama JobPostingsAPI](https://glama.ai/mcp/connectors/com.jobpostingsapi/jobs-ats-greenhouse-workday-mcp); [mcpservers.org](https://mcpservers.org/servers/anshumanatrey/ats-jobs-mcp)
- Ashby Public Job Posting API: `GET api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/{JOB_BOARD_NAME}?includeCompensation=true`. It needs no auth for reads, is read-only, returns all published postings for one board in one response, and can include compensation — [Ashby developer docs](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/public-job-posting-api); [DEV write-up](https://dev.to/zsevic/integration-with-ashby-public-jobs-api-34n6)
- Recruiter-side agent interfaces (Greenhouse MCP, Ashby MCP, Workday Agent Gateway with MCP + A2A) are claimed by one blog and unverified — [HeroHunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/agentic-ats-2026-greenhouse-vs-ashby-vs-workday/)
- Agent-to-agent job matchmakers (JobClaw pre-launch, AgentHire unverified) are covered in the earlier report — [JobClaw](https://jobclaw.org/); [AgentHire](https://agenhire.com/)

### Inferences
- Greenhouse Job Board API and Lever Postings API: based on prior knowledge and *not re-verified this session* (developers.greenhouse.io was unreachable), Greenhouse exposes public unauthenticated GETs at `boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{board_token}/jobs` (with `?content=true`). Application submission is a POST that requires the employer's Job Board API key (HTTP Basic). Lever exposes public `GET api.lever.co/v0/postings/{site}?mode=json`, and its apply POST requires an employer-issued API key. **Implication:** a hub can *ingest* public jobs freely, but can't *submit* applications into these ATSs without each employer's key. That supports a "claim your listing / connect your ATS" employer onboarding flow. Verify both docs and terms before building.
- schema.org `JobPosting` (Google for Jobs structured data) is the de-facto public description format. A hub's `job_offer` intent schema should map 1:1 to JobPosting fields (title, baseSalary, employmentType, jobLocationType=TELECOMMUTE, applicantLocationRequirements, validThrough, directApply) so it can import and export cheaply. This is based on prior knowledge and was not fetched this session.
- HR Open Standards (Recruiting / Candidate / PositionOpening JSON schemas) is the only formal industry interchange standard for applications. Adopting its Candidate schema for the `job_seek` intent would ease ATS write-back. Not verified this session.
- The gap is a *write-path* standard: agent-submitted, identity-bound, structured applications that an ATS accepts as first-class rather than as spam. No one appears to own it, and it is AgentsHub's clearest wedge in hiring.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the current text of the Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby API terms of use (commercial aggregation and redistribution rights). Must be checked with counsel before ingesting at scale.
- Found no evidence of any "agent-ready job board" certification or HR Open Standards work on agent-submitted applications in 2026. Absence of evidence, not proof.
- Did not verify Indeed's or LinkedIn's posture on third-party agents reading their job data. Both prohibit scraping in their ToS (known background, not fetched).

## 4. Personal and enterprise agent adoption in 2026: how many agents could connect

### Takeaway
Plenty of people *use* AI assistants (ChatGPT has about 1–1.2B weekly users), but far fewer run *agents that act*. ChatGPT's agentic products passed about 10M weekly users (Jul 2026), and OpenClaw has an estimated 2–3.2M MAU (unverified). Enterprises are early: about 17% have deployed agents (Gartner) and about 60–71% plan to. Only 3–9% of consumers accept fully autonomous agent action, but more than 50% accept it with approve or undo controls. A realistic addressable base of agents that could call an MCP or A2A hub today is in the low tens of millions of individuals, and it is heavily technical.

### Cited Findings
**Consumer and personal agents**
- ChatGPT: 1B weekly users (OpenAI, 6 Aug 2026). About 1.2B was reported around DevDay (29 Sept 2026), mainly via aggregators **[secondary]** — [Wikipedia: ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT); [TechnologyChecker](https://technologychecker.io/blog/chatgpt-statistics)
- OpenAI's agentic products (two combined, including ChatGPT Work) passed **10M weekly users**, reported 21 Jul 2026. This counts weekly actives, not seats — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-agents-10-million-chatgpt-work). Codex alone had more than 2M weekly actives by Mar 2026 — [Wikipedia: Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_(AI_agent))

### Inferences
- **Rough sizing of "agents that could plausibly connect to a hub via MCP/A2A" (order of magnitude, my estimate):** about 10M weekly users of OpenAI agentic products, about 2–3M OpenClaw MAU (unverified), plus Claude, Gemini and Copilot agent users (not quantified). That gives roughly 15–30M individuals worldwide whose agent could call a remote MCP tool today. Only a small fraction are job-seeking at any moment: at a ~5–10% annual active-search rate that is about 1–3M, skewed heavily toward tech workers. On the employer side, the reachable agents are recruiter-side tools (LinkedIn Hiring Assistant, Juicebox, Ashby or Greenhouse AI, Workday/Paradox), which are walled gardens. Realistic employer-agent connections early on will come through an ATS integration or a hub-hosted employer agent, not BYO agents.
- The delegation surveys strongly support **human-in-the-loop by default**: more than 50% accept approve or undo, under 10% accept full autonomy. Product framing should be "your agent finds and pre-negotiates; you approve every hand-off".
- The enterprise 17% deployed versus 21% with governance gap is a selling point for a hub that provides audit logs and verification out of the box.

### Gaps

- I found no credible count of live MCP-capable or A2A-capable agents. Registry counts (Agentverse ~3M and similar, from the earlier report) measure listings, not active agents.
- I found no Pew data.

## 5. Best beachhead niche within hiring

### Takeaway
Evidence favours **AI/ML and senior software engineers hiring at AI-forward startups (remote, US time zones)** as the first beachhead. Both sides are agent-savvy (they are the OpenClaw, Codex and Claude user base), the roles are scarce and slow to fill, and startup employers already use Ashby or Greenhouse, whose public board APIs allow seeding. Israeli tech is a strong *secondary or community* wedge: there is now oversupply (0.95 openings per seeker) and a tight network, but the market is small. Hourly/frontline is the wrong first niche: Workday-Paradox already owns conversational automation there, and candidates are the least likely to run personal agents.

### Cited Findings
- AI engineer demand: about 143% YoY growth in postings and about 3.2 open AI/ML roles per qualified candidate **[staffing vendor]**. Senior AI reqs take 90–120 days in-house, with some sources reporting more than 6 months. LinkedIn reportedly ranks AI Engineer as the #1 fastest-growing US title. ### Inferences
- **Remote AI/ML and senior SWE (US-first, global remote)**: high value per hire (recruiter fees of 15–25% of six-figure salaries), acute scarcity on the employer side, and an agent-native user base on both sides. Employers are on Ashby or Greenhouse (public APIs make seeding easy) and are already comfortable with AI sourcing tools. Main risks: candidates are passive and heavily courted, so the hub must offer them privacy and "only contact me if salary ≥ X" control. LinkedIn Hiring Assistant 2 also targets exactly these profiles.
- **Israeli tech** works as a community-led pilot rather than the main market. Seekers now outnumber openings, so the pain sits on the *candidate* side (oversupply, brain drain), which makes candidate recruitment easy and employer liquidity hard. Its value is a dense, reachable network for a founder with access, plus cross-border remote matching to US employers.
- **Hourly/frontline** has the biggest volume and the clearest application-spam pain. But candidates rarely run personal agents, and the incumbent (Workday/Paradox) already automates the employer side. A hub there would mostly have to host agents for candidates, which defeats the bring-your-own-agent thesis. Revisit it later via SMS or WhatsApp-hosted lite agents.
- A suggested sharper wedge: **"AI engineers and AI-forward startups"**. The hub's first employer customers are the companies that build or heavily use agents, so they are the most likely to connect their own agents via MCP or A2A and to tolerate an early protocol.

### Gaps
- Agent-savviness by occupation is not measured directly. The inference relies on OpenClaw, Codex and Claude user bases skewing technical, and no survey quantifies agent use among job seekers by role.
- I found no 2026 hourly-hiring benchmark (time to hire, applicants per hourly role) and no independent Paradox outcome data.
- AI-engineer shortage ratios come from staffing vendors and conflict (3.2:1, 3.4:1, 1.6M vs 518k). Treat them as directional only.
